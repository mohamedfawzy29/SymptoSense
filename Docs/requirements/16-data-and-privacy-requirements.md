# Data & Privacy Requirements

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft — Team Review Required  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-15

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the requirements governing how SymptoSense collects, processes, stores, protects, accesses, modifies, and deletes user data.

The purpose is to ensure that SymptoSense:

- Collects only the data necessary to provide its supported functionality.
- Protects user and assessment information.
- Separates guest and authenticated-user data appropriately.
- Limits access to sensitive information.
- Defines how assessment data moves through its lifecycle.
- Controls how AI and external services interact with user data.
- Provides appropriate mechanisms for data modification and deletion.
- Maintains traceability without collecting unnecessary personal information.

These requirements are intended to guide the system architecture, implementation, security controls, and future privacy decisions.

---

## 2. Privacy Principle

SymptoSense shall follow the principle of data minimization.

The system shall collect, process, and retain only the data necessary to provide the supported functionality.

The system shall not collect personal or medical information solely because it may be useful in the future.

The following principle shall apply:

> Collect only what is needed, use it only for an approved purpose, protect it appropriately, and retain it only as long as necessary.

---

## 3. Data Categories

SymptoSense may process several categories of data.

These may include:

- Account Data
- Assessment Data
- Symptom Data
- User Responses
- Assessment Results
- Safety and Urgency Information
- Assessment History
- Draft Assessment Data
- AI Processing Data
- System and Audit Data
- Technical Session Data

Each category shall have a defined purpose and appropriate access and retention behavior.

---

## 4. Account Data

For authenticated users, the system may store information required to create and manage the user's account.

Account data may include:

- User identifier
- Authentication information
- Email address when required
- Account status
- Account creation information
- Account-related preferences when supported

The system shall not store additional personal information unless required by an approved feature.

Authentication credentials shall not be stored in plain text.

---

## 5. Assessment Data

Assessment data represents information provided or generated during an assessment.

It may include:

- Symptoms
- Symptom locations
- Symptom severity
- Symptom duration
- Associated symptoms
- User answers
- Relevant context
- Clarifications
- User corrections
- Assessment state
- Assessment result
- Urgency level
- Applicable safety findings

Assessment data shall be treated as sensitive information and shall be protected accordingly.

---

## 6. Symptom Data

Symptom information shall be stored only when necessary for the supported assessment functionality.

The system may store structured symptom information such as:

- Symptom identifier
- Symptom name
- Body location
- Severity
- Duration
- Characteristics
- Associated symptoms
- Status of the information
- Source of the information when required

The system shall distinguish between information explicitly provided by the user and information generated or interpreted by the system.

---

## 7. User-Provided Information

The system shall preserve the distinction between:

- Information explicitly provided by the user.
- Information interpreted from the user's response.
- Information derived by the Assessment Engine.
- Information generated by AI.

The system shall not represent an AI interpretation as though it were directly stated by the user.

Where practical, important assessment information should maintain its origin or source.

---

## 8. Information States

Assessment-related information may have different states.

The system shall support distinguishing between:

- Known
- Unknown
- Not Provided
- Unclear
- Conflicting
- Confirmed

Missing information shall not automatically be stored as a negative value.

For example:

    Fever = Unknown

shall not be converted into:

    Fever = False

unless an approved rule explicitly defines that behavior.

---

## 9. Guest User Data

SymptoSense shall support assessments initiated by guest users according to the approved Business Rules.

A guest user shall not automatically receive the same persistent data capabilities as an authenticated user.

The guest assessment flow shall prioritize data minimization.

The exact persistence behavior for guest assessment data shall be determined by the final architecture and privacy review.

The initial requirement is:

> Guest assessments shall not be treated as persistent user-owned assessment history.

---

## 10. Guest Assessment Session

Guest assessment information may exist temporarily during an active assessment session when required to provide the assessment functionality.

Temporary guest data shall not automatically become part of a permanent user profile.

When the guest assessment session ends, the system shall follow the approved retention behavior for temporary session data.

The final retention period shall be defined by the team after reviewing security, privacy, and technical requirements.

---

## 11. Authenticated User Data

Authenticated users may have persistent data associated with their account when required by supported features.

Persistent user data may include:

- Account information
- Completed assessments
- Assessment history
- Draft assessments
- User preferences
- Other approved account-related information

Persistent data shall always remain associated with the appropriate user account.

---

## 12. Assessment Draft Data

According to the approved Business Rules, authenticated users may resume incomplete assessments.

Therefore, the system may persist Draft Assessment data for authenticated users.

A Draft may contain:

- Previously provided symptoms
- Previous answers
- Current assessment state
- Required information already collected
- Current assessment context
- Information necessary to resume the assessment

A Draft shall not be treated as a Completed Assessment.

A Draft shall not:

- Appear as a completed result in assessment history.
- Produce a final assessment result.
- Be treated as a completed medical assessment.

---

## 13. Draft Auto-Save

Authenticated assessment drafts shall be saved automatically when required to support the Resume Assessment functionality.

The system should save sufficient information to allow the user to continue from the latest valid saved state.

The system shall avoid unnecessarily storing duplicate or transient information that is not required to resume the assessment.

---

## 14. Assessment History

Completed assessments may be stored as part of the user's assessment history when the feature is enabled.

Assessment history may contain:

- Assessment identifier
- Completion date
- Symptoms
- Relevant assessment information
- Assessment result
- Urgency level
- Relevant safety information
- Assessment and medical-rule versions when required for traceability

Assessment history shall remain associated with the correct authenticated user.

---

## 15. Draft vs Completed Data

The system shall clearly distinguish between Draft and Completed assessment data.

The distinction shall be maintained at both the application and data levels where appropriate.

The system shall not expose a Draft as though it were a completed assessment.

The system shall not create a final result solely because a Draft exists.

---

## 16. User Corrections

Users shall be able to correct supported assessment information before completion according to the Business Rules.

When information is corrected:

    Previous Information
            ↓
    User Correction
            ↓
    Updated Assessment Context
            ↓
    Assessment Re-evaluation

The current accepted value shall be used by the Assessment Engine for subsequent assessment decisions.

---

## 17. Historical Traceability of Corrections

Where required for assessment integrity, the system should maintain sufficient information to determine that an assessment value was changed.

The system shall not retain previous values solely for historical purposes unless there is an approved business, safety, auditing, or technical reason to do so.

If previous values are retained, access to them shall be appropriately restricted.

---

## 18. Assessment Result Data

Assessment results shall contain only information necessary to present and maintain the result.

The result may include:

- Assessment outcome
- Urgency level
- Possible explanations
- Safety information
- Relevant findings
- Result timestamp
- Assessment version information

The system shall not store unnecessary AI-generated text when the same information can be represented in a structured form.

---

## 19. AI Data Processing

AI may process user-provided information for approved purposes such as:

- Understanding natural-language responses
- Extracting structured symptom information
- Generating natural-language questions
- Understanding user corrections
- Explaining assessment results

AI processing shall remain within the boundaries defined by the AI Requirements.

AI shall not independently become the authoritative source for the final medical assessment.

---

## 20. AI Data Minimization

Only the information necessary for the specific AI operation should be provided to the AI component.

The system should avoid sending unrelated personal or assessment information to the AI service.

For example, if the AI only needs to interpret a user's answer to a symptom question, unrelated account information should not be included.

---

## 21. AI and Personal Identifiers

Personal identifiers shall not be provided to an AI service unless they are necessary for an approved system function.

When possible, AI processing should use only the minimum assessment context required for the operation.

The system should separate:

    User Identity
          ↓
    Assessment Information
          ↓
    AI Processing

when the AI operation does not require knowledge of the user's identity.

---

## 22. AI Output Handling

AI-generated output shall not automatically be treated as trusted system data.

AI output that affects assessment processing shall be validated before being accepted by the system.

For example:

    AI
      ↓
    Structured Output
      ↓
    Validation
      ↓
    Assessment Context

Invalid or unsupported AI output shall not be silently accepted.

---

## 23. External AI Services

If SymptoSense uses a third-party AI service, the system shall evaluate:

- What data is sent.
- Why the data is sent.
- Where the data is processed.
- Whether the provider retains the data.
- Whether the provider uses submitted data for other purposes.
- Available security controls.
- Available privacy controls.
- Applicable contractual or service requirements.

The final external AI provider and associated privacy configuration shall be an architectural and product decision.

---

## 24. Third-Party Services

Any third-party service that processes user or assessment data shall be identified and reviewed.

The system shall document, where applicable:

- Service purpose
- Data shared
- Reason for sharing
- Data retention behavior
- Access controls
- Security mechanisms
- Relevant privacy considerations

Third-party integrations shall not receive more information than required for their intended function.

---

## 25. Data Access Control

Access to user data shall follow the principle of least privilege.

A component, service, or user shall only have access to the information required for its authorized purpose.

Access shall be restricted according to:

- User identity
- Authentication state
- Authorization level
- Resource ownership
- Service responsibility

---

## 26. User Data Isolation

Authenticated users shall only be able to access their own personal and assessment data unless an explicitly authorized administrative or system function requires otherwise.

The system shall prevent one user's assessment data from being exposed to another user.

This requirement shall apply to:

- Assessment history
- Draft assessments
- Assessment results
- Personal information
- Other user-owned resources

---

## 27. Guest Data Isolation

Guest assessment data shall remain isolated from authenticated user accounts unless an explicit supported flow associates the assessment with an authenticated user.

The system shall not accidentally attach one guest session's assessment information to another user.

---

## 28. Authentication and Authorization

Access to persistent user-owned data shall require appropriate authentication and authorization.

Authentication shall establish the identity of the user.

Authorization shall determine whether that user is allowed to access the requested resource.

The system shall not rely solely on client-provided identifiers to determine resource ownership.

---

## 29. Sensitive Data Protection

Sensitive user and assessment information shall be protected during storage and transmission.

The exact security mechanisms shall be defined by the system security architecture.

At minimum, the implementation shall consider:

- Encryption in transit
- Appropriate protection at rest
- Secure authentication
- Authorization
- Secret management
- Access control
- Secure logging

---

## 30. Secrets and Credentials

The system shall not store sensitive credentials or service secrets directly in application source code.

Examples include:

- Database credentials
- API keys
- AI service credentials
- Authentication secrets
- Encryption keys

Secrets shall be managed using an appropriate secure configuration or secret-management mechanism.

---

## 31. Logging

System logs shall not unnecessarily contain sensitive user or medical information.

Logs should contain enough technical information to support:

- Debugging
- Monitoring
- Error investigation
- Security investigation
- System operation

Sensitive values should be excluded, minimized, or appropriately protected.

---

## 32. AI and Logging

AI requests and responses shall not automatically be stored in application logs.

If AI interactions need to be logged for debugging or auditing, the system shall minimize sensitive information and define the applicable retention and access rules.

---

## 33. Auditability

The system should maintain sufficient technical information to investigate important system actions.

Audit information may include:

- Assessment lifecycle events
- Important state changes
- Result generation
- Rule or knowledge versions
- Security-relevant events
- Data modification events

Audit data shall not contain unnecessary sensitive information.

---

## 34. Data Modification

The system shall support modification of user-owned data where the applicable feature allows modification.

Assessment information may be modified according to the Business Rules before completion.

Completed assessment records shall not be arbitrarily modified in a way that compromises historical integrity.

If modification of completed records is supported, the behavior shall be explicitly defined.

---

## 35. Data Deletion

The system shall provide an appropriate mechanism for deleting user-owned data where required by the supported account and privacy functionality.

Deletion behavior shall clearly distinguish between:

- Account deletion
- Assessment deletion
- Draft deletion
- Temporary session data
- Technical or audit records

The final deletion rules and retention periods shall be defined by the team after reviewing applicable legal, security, operational, and product requirements.

---

## 36. Account Deletion

If account deletion is supported, the system shall define what happens to data associated with the account.

The deletion process shall identify:

- Account data
- Draft assessments
- Assessment history
- Assessment results
- User preferences
- Associated application data

The system shall also identify any information that may require separate retention for legitimate operational or legal reasons.

The exact retention exceptions shall be determined during the privacy and legal review.

---

## 37. Data Retention

The system shall define retention periods based on the purpose of each data category.

Retention shall not be unlimited by default.

The team shall define appropriate retention behavior for:

- Guest session data
- Draft assessments
- Completed assessments
- Assessment history
- AI processing data
- Application logs
- Audit data
- Security logs

Retention periods shall be documented before production deployment.

---

## 38. Data Lifecycle

User and assessment data shall follow a defined lifecycle.

The general lifecycle is:

    Collection
       ↓
    Processing
       ↓
    Temporary Storage / Persistent Storage
       ↓
    Use
       ↓
    Retention
       ↓
    Deletion / Expiration

Each data category should have a defined lifecycle appropriate to its purpose.

---

## 39. Data Minimization in Storage

The system shall avoid storing information that is no longer required for the supported functionality.

When a piece of data is no longer necessary and there is no approved retention requirement, it should be eligible for deletion or expiration.

---

## 40. Data Export

If data export is supported, the exported data shall contain only information associated with the requesting user and only information that the user is authorized to receive.

The system shall prevent another user's data from being included in an export.

The final export format and scope shall be defined as part of the account and privacy functionality.

---

## 41. Data Portability

If data portability is included in the product scope, the system shall provide user-owned data in a structured and reasonably usable format.

The exact scope and format shall be determined during product and privacy review.

---

## 42. Data Ownership and Responsibility

The system shall maintain a clear distinction between:

- User-provided information
- System-generated assessment information
- AI-generated communication
- Medical knowledge
- Technical system data

The system shall not represent generated or inferred information as though it were directly provided by the user.

---

## 43. Medical Data Sensitivity

Assessment information may contain sensitive health-related information.

Therefore, the system shall apply appropriate protection to assessment data regardless of whether the user is authenticated or a guest.

Guest status shall not be treated as a reason to reduce the security requirements for sensitive information.

---

## 44. Data Used by the Assessment Engine

The Assessment Engine shall receive only the information required to perform its responsibilities.

The engine shall not require unnecessary personal identifiers to perform assessment logic when those identifiers do not affect the assessment.

Where possible:

    User Identity
          ↓
    Account / Authorization Layer

    Assessment Context
          ↓
    Assessment Engine

This separation reduces unnecessary exposure of personal information.

---

## 45. Data Used by the 3D Body Component

The 3D Body component shall receive only the information required to support the approved body-location interaction.

The component shall not independently receive unrelated personal or account information.

The exact data exchange between the 3D Body component and the Assessment system shall be defined in the 3D Body Requirements.

---

## 46. Data Consistency

The system shall maintain consistency between stored assessment information and the current assessment state.

When an assessment is updated, dependent data shall be updated or re-evaluated as required.

For example:

    User Correction
          ↓
    Assessment Context Updated
          ↓
    Assessment Re-evaluated
          ↓
    Stored State Updated

The system shall avoid retaining contradictory active versions of the same assessment information.

---

## 47. Data Integrity

The system shall protect assessment and user data from unauthorized or accidental modification.

Important assessment records should maintain sufficient integrity controls to detect unexpected changes where required.

---

## 48. Privacy by Design

Privacy considerations shall be included during system design rather than added only after implementation.

New features that introduce new categories of user or assessment data shall be reviewed before implementation.

The review should consider:

- What data is collected.
- Why it is required.
- Where it is stored.
- Who can access it.
- How long it is retained.
- Whether it is shared externally.
- How it can be deleted.

---

## 49. New Data Collection

Any new feature that requires collecting a new category of personal or assessment data shall require review before the data is introduced into the system.

The feature shall document:

- Purpose
- Data category
- Collection method
- Storage requirements
- Access requirements
- Retention requirements
- Deletion behavior
- External sharing, if any

---

## 50. Privacy and Security Failure Handling

If the system detects a failure that may expose or incorrectly process user data, the system shall:

- Prevent further unauthorized processing where possible.
- Record an appropriate technical event.
- Avoid exposing sensitive information in error messages.
- Follow the system's incident-handling process.

Specific incident response procedures shall be defined separately from this document.

---

## 51. Data Processing Boundaries

Each system component shall process only the data required for its responsibility.

The intended boundary is:

    User
      ↓
    Application
      ↓
    ┌─────────────────────┐
    │ Appropriate Service │
    └─────────────────────┘
      ↓
    Required Data Only

Components shall not receive unrestricted access to the complete user profile or complete assessment history unless explicitly required.

---

## 52. Privacy and AI Boundary

The AI layer shall not be granted unrestricted access to the user's complete account or assessment history by default.

AI shall receive only the information required for the current operation.

For example:

    Current User Answer
          ↓
    AI Processing

rather than:

    Complete User Account
          ↓
    AI Processing

unless a specific approved feature requires broader context.

---

## 53. Data Retention by Processing Purpose

Where third-party or AI services are used, the system shall distinguish between:

- Data required for real-time processing.
- Data required for application functionality.
- Data retained for assessment history.
- Data retained for technical monitoring.
- Data retained by external service providers.

The system shall avoid retaining data simply because an external provider technically allows it.

---

## 54. Privacy Requirements for MVP

The MVP shall prioritize the following privacy capabilities:

- Secure authenticated user data
- Appropriate guest data handling
- Assessment data isolation
- Draft data protection
- Assessment history protection
- Minimum necessary AI data sharing
- Sensitive-data-aware logging
- Access control
- Basic data deletion capability where included in MVP scope
- Defined retention behavior before production deployment

---

## 55. Open Privacy Decisions

The following decisions remain subject to team review:

### 55.1 Guest Session Persistence

The team shall determine the exact server-side persistence behavior and retention period for guest assessment sessions.

### 55.2 Assessment History Retention

The team shall determine how long completed assessments should remain available to authenticated users.

### 55.3 Draft Retention

The team shall determine how long an incomplete authenticated assessment Draft should remain available.

### 55.4 AI Provider Data Retention

If a third-party AI provider is used, the team shall review and approve the provider's applicable data-retention behavior and privacy configuration.

### 55.5 Account Deletion Behavior

The team shall determine the exact behavior for data associated with deleted accounts, including any approved retention exceptions.

### 55.6 Data Export

The team shall determine whether data export is included in the MVP.

### 55.7 Data Portability

The team shall determine whether data portability is included in the MVP.

---

## 56. Architectural Decision Boundary

This document defines data and privacy requirements but does not mandate a specific database, cloud provider, AI provider, authentication technology, encryption technology, or infrastructure architecture.

Those implementation decisions shall be documented separately.

The selected architecture must satisfy the requirements defined in this document.
